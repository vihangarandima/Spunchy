## 1. Logo colours

|---|---|---|---|
| **Spunchy Maroon** | `#7A1523` | Field of the logo roundel, the ceiling raft in store, the "tab" that holds the logo on every IG post | ~90% |
| **Swash Gold** | `#F7B836` | The swash and the "y" | accent |

## 2. Logo shape language

- **Brush-script wordmark.** Fast, slanted (~12° right lean), with thick-thin stroke contrast as if drawn with a chisel brush. Confident and hand-made, not calligraphic or fussy.
- **The swash.** A long gold ribbon that starts from the "y" descender, sweeps left under the word and kicks up at the end. It is the most ownable element: a single continuous **S-curve that speeds up**.
- **Roundel container.** On social the wordmark sits in a maroon circle with a thin white ring. On posts it sits in a maroon **tab with rounded bottom corners** hanging from the top edge, like a ribbon tag on a cake box.
- **Rounded but not bubbly.** Terminals are rounded and joins are soft. Nothing is geometric or sharp.

How this carries into the site:

| Element | Echo of the logo |
|---|---|
| Section dividers, wipes | The swash S-curve, drawn as an SVG path and animated along its length |
| Image masks | Roundel (circle) and the hanging tab (rounded-bottom rectangle) |
| Cursor | A small gold swash "comet" that stretches in the direction of travel |
| Loader / intro | The swash drawing itself under the wordmark |
| Buttons | Pill shapes with a 12° skewed hover fill, matching the script's lean |

## 3. Recurring visual patterns (Instagram and in store)

- **Maroon logo tab, top-centre,** on almost every post. The brand frame is consistent.
- **Two post registers:**
  1. *Promo cards*: a cream/blush background or dark food photography, a heavy rounded display face ("VIRAL CHOCO BRICK", "TANDOORI CHICKEN BUN"), a big price burst ("Rs. 350", "Rs. 290/-"), and the outlet list in a strip at the bottom.
  2. *Creator reels*: handheld, real-life eating shots in the Kottawa store (maroon chairs and wood walls visible), Sinhala text hooks, lots of close-up cross-sections and "cheese pull" moments.
- **Food shot as a cross-section.** Products are cut open to show the filling (choco brick, chicken roll, the samosa-style buns on the "Soft, Fluffy & Fresh" poster). This is the brand's signature food shot.
- **In-store posters:** black backgrounds, product lit from above, a serif headline with one word swapped into a bold display ("Cake is the language of **Love**", "Soft, Fluffy & **Fresh**", "A slice of heaven in every bite", "**Savory**: crispy, crunchy, crave-worthy").
- **Store materials:** vertical warm-oak plank walls, spotlights on black track, maroon ceiling raft, glass cake cabinets glowing from inside, orange Spunchy boxes stacked on top. The cabinet glow against dark wood is the most cinematic thing in the photos.
- **Cake decoration:** cream buttercream with rosettes in pink, ivory and plum, which sit right inside the logo palette.

## 4. Tone of voice

- **Young, cheeky, local.** Sinhala and English mixed in one line ("කොට්ටාවේ මෙච්චර අඩුවට cakes කන්න තැනක්!", roughly "a place in Kottawa to eat cakes this cheap!"), meme formats ("I'm literally just a bun"), payday jokes ("Food before $, after payday").
- **Indulgent and sensory** on the posters: soft, fluffy, crispy, crunchy, crave-worthy, a slice of heaven.
- **Value-confident.** Prices shown proudly and big.
- **Inclusive and open.** "Calling all foodies & content creators" and "Some things are just better together".

For the site: keep the posters' sensory lines as the headline voice and the Instagram cheek as the micro-copy. The premium feel comes from craft and polish, not from stiffness. Every line has to be either Spunchy's own words or approved by the client.

## 5. Product hero items

By how often they appear and how prominently they are pushed:

1. **Celebration cakes.** Rosette buttercream cakes, gateaux and rainbow cakes. The cabinet is the centrepiece of every store.
2. **Choco Brick.** The "viral" product (Rs. 350), with its own campaign.
3. **Savory buns and rolls.** Tandoori chicken bun (Rs. 290), smoke chicken roll, triangle buns, cutlets.
4. **Pastry slices.** Fruit tart (Rs. 290), gateau slices, mousse, blueberry and coffee slices.
5. **Bread and packed bakes.** Loaves, sugar bun packs, cookies.

Real prices exist for Fruit Tart 290/- (store photo), Choco Brick 350 and Tandoori Chicken Bun 290 (Instagram). All others need confirming.

## 6. Audience

- **Primary:** 18–30 year olds in the southern Colombo suburbs (Kottawa, Maharagama, Piliyandala, Boralesgamuwa) who are budget-aware, social-first, order on PickMe and follow food creators.
- **Secondary:** families buying celebration cakes for birthdays and occasions, and office buyers ordering savoury platters.
- **Gifting:** the boxes and rosette cakes are made for it.

## 7. Genre decision

**Maroon Night: a cinematic patisserie with a playful swash.** This is a blend of *Indulgent cinematic* and *Joyful premium*.

- **The stage is maroon, not black.** The logo is 90% maroon, the store ceiling is maroon, and the posters are dark. A deep maroon "night" background lets products glow the way they do in the cake cabinets. No other Sri Lankan bakery owns this colour. Black would make Spunchy look like any luxury chocolatier; cream would make it look like every bakery template (and like the generic AI default).
- **Light is gold.** The swash gold works as light: rim light on products, the line that draws the story, the cursor, the price stamps.
- **Food is the hero, and it is cut open.** The signature cross-section shot becomes the scroll mechanic: products split, reveal their layers and fillings.
- **The motion has the swash's energy.** Fast-in, soft-out curves with a little overshoot, like a brush stroke. This gives the playfulness of their Instagram without looking childish.
- **Local pride, quietly.** Bilingual moments (Sinhala headline flourishes), real outlet names, PickMe, rupee prices. These make it unmistakably a Sri Lankan brand without folk-pattern clichés.

Why not the others:

- *Pure indulgent black-and-gold* would erase the brand's warmth and humour, and it is visually generic.
- *Bright candy 3D* would fit the reels but fight the "Premium Pastry & Bakery Brand" positioning.
- *Artisan heritage* (kraft paper, rustic) doesn't match a brand whose stores are modern, glossy and spotlit.
